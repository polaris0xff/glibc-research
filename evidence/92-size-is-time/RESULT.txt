THE ANSWER
  ⛔ NO, NOT IN ANY SENSE THAT MATTERS.

  The image grew 29.60x and the file count grew 137.99x. Both moved cold
  start by about 1.05x, at or just above the instrument floor --
  and two consecutive runs disagreed about whether the byte axis
  crosses that floor at all (1.04x vs floor 1.06, then 1.05x vs
  floor 1.03), while the A/A control held at 1.01-1.02 in both.
  So the effect is real and it is SMALL.

  Costed: 0.0243 ms per MiB of image. Carried across the 196 MiB that
  separates our kdenlive bundle (398 MB) from the competitor's
  (192 MB), that is 4.8 ms -- 3.7% of the SMALLEST cold-start gap
  ever observed between them (129 ms; the other three runs in
  corrections.md C23 are 2,019 / 239 / 4,758 ms, which make the
  share smaller still).

  CONSEQUENCE: the byte levers (--cut, --fixpoint, the debloat
  rules) cannot close the gap. Under the 2026-09-03c ruling that
  makes them unscored AND unpromising, and the remaining work is
  the mount itself -- PROGRESS.md N6.
