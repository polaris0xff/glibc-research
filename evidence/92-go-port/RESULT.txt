⭐ THE DEFECT: A PLAN THAT IS NOT A PROPERTY OF THE GRAPH
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
An output store path can be claimed by MORE THAN ONE derivation — two .drv
files that build to the same output are equivalent, and a real graph has
several. In git's graph, 14 output basenames are claimed twice, among them

  /nix/store/anri00q3zrwkxdnch0vic10yy9kykgfp-perl-5.42.0
      out  bgz1mxqdhjymq3cabdhavp0s3hqjqn3h-perl-5.42.0.drv
      out  fql06jx5r03zc8zc2sxgy3km0r1vikhp-perl-5.42.0.drv

`nix-plan.py` builds its output index with last-writer-wins over the
document's own key order, so which of the two lands in `deps[].drv` depends
on the order nix happened to print them in.

THE CONTROL, and it is what turns this from a choice into a defect. The same
graph, its `derivations` object shuffled ten ways, content identical:

  python3 tool/nix-plan.py git ... :  4 of 10 -> plan A
                                      6 of 10 -> plan B
  pgb nix plan git ...             : 10 of 10 -> plan A

⭐ The Go planner sorts the claimants and takes the lowest store path, so the
plan is a property of the graph. `--log debug` reports how many output paths
had more than one claimant; `--log trace` names each.

⚠ Both answers are BUILDABLE — the two derivations produce the same output —
so nothing built from either plan was wrong. What was wrong is that one
attribute had two plans and nothing said so.

ZSTD - decoded in Go, differential against the reference encoder
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
⛔ WHY IT IS A GATE AT ALL. The pinned build environment carries no zstd
binary. The retired Python reached libzstd.so.1 through ctypes; CGO_ENABLED=0
has no equivalent, so `pgb nix build` inside the environment stopped at
"zstd-compressed and no zstd decoder on PATH" -- experiment 88 arm 5.

internal/zstd decodes RFC 8878 with nothing outside the standard library.

  corpus:  120 frames from 12 inputs -- /usr/bin/gcc, libc.so.6, pgb itself,
           /usr/share/dict/words, GPL-3, a build log, 3 MB of urandom, and
           six synthetic shapes -- at levels 1,2,3,5,8,12,16,19, plus
           --ultra -22 --long=27 and -3 --no-check.
  result:  120 of 120 byte-identical to the input, read whole AND one byte
           at a time.
  damage:  572 truncations, every one refused. 400 flipped bytes: none
           decoded to different content without an error.

  Carried offline as `pgb selftest zstd`: 8 frames and the generators for
  their plaintexts, 22 cases.

⚠ xz is still decoded by the xz command. cache.nixos.org serves .nar.xz for
older paths; the build environment has xz and a target rootfs does not, so an
xz NAR fetched from inside a target would still stop. Stated, not hidden.


CI - green again, after three rounds of repair
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
⛔ CI HAD BEEN RED FOR EVERY COMMIT OF THE PORT AND NOTHING SAID SO. `pgb` is
a built binary now and is not committed, so `build`, `verify-docker` and
`probe-host` each did `actions/checkout` and then ran `./pgb` against
nothing; `probe-host` also ran `sh -n` over it.

A `toolchain` job builds one static pgb with setup-go and hands it to the
others as an artefact. ⭐ That is the distribution claim under test: the
binary built on the runner is then used, unchanged and with nothing installed
for it, inside the pinned Debian 12 container.
